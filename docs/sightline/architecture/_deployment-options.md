Sightline comes in three deployment options. They differ in where Sightline runs, who operates it and what support applies.

| Option | Where it runs | Who operates it | Support |
|---|---|---|---|
| Local Community Sightline | In VS Code on your own computer | You | Community |
| Self-Hosted Sightline | On infrastructure that you run | You | Documentation only |
| Corvis Sightline | On a deployment that Corvis runs for you | Corvis | Full |

## Local Community Sightline

Local Community Sightline is the Sightline extension installed in your own copy of VS Code. Your models stay in the workspace on your computer. See [Install Sightline](/docs/sightline/install/extensions).

Support for this option comes from the community.

## Self-Hosted Sightline

Self-Hosted Sightline runs on infrastructure that you operate. Your models stay on that infrastructure, which suits an organisation that must keep its models in a location it controls.

This option has documentation only. Corvis does not provide support for a Self-Hosted Sightline deployment. See [Run Sightline on your own infrastructure](/docs/sightline/install/self-hosted) for the setup.

## Corvis Sightline

Corvis runs Corvis Sightline for you. Each customer has a separate deployment.

Corvis supports this option fully.

## How the licence applies

All three options use the same licence. A licence names the apps that it unlocks, and an app that the licence does not name does not save. Viewing and export stay usable.

A hosted deployment reads one licence that its operator provides. It applies the licence to every save that a signed-in user makes.

See [Licence](/docs/sightline/legal/licence) for the terms and [Licensing](/docs/sightline/usage/shared/licensing) for what each app shows.
